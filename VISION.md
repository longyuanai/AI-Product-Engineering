## Mission

Provide a vendor-neutral, evidence-driven standard that helps AI-era teams build complete products — not just write code.

## Vision

A future where any team using AI-assisted development can ship a product that has been discovered, validated, built, and grown under a shared, transparent, auditable workflow.

## Problem

Modern AI tools accelerate code generation, but most teams still lack:

- A consistent process for deciding what to build.
- Evidence-based mechanisms for validating ideas before code.
- Cross-functional alignment among product, business, and engineering.
- A shared vocabulary for stages, gates, and deliverables.

The result is fast code shipping into a product strategy that is still undefined.

## Target Users

- Small AI-assisted product teams (typically 2–10 people) that need a shared playbook.
- Independent builders and studios shipping AI-powered products.
- Larger organizations standardizing AI-assisted product development across multiple teams.
- Educators and researchers studying AI-era product engineering practice.

## Goals

- Make the lifecycle stages, gates, and artifacts explicit.
- Make evidence the default currency for product, business, and technical decisions.
- Provide enough structure to be useful, and enough flexibility to remain vendor-neutral.
- Be transparent about provenance, citations, and limitations.

## Non-Goals

- Replacing general product management frameworks (Lean, Shape Up, etc.).
- Replacing engineering quality standards (testing, security, etc.).
- Promoting any specific vendor, model, or tool.
- Claiming any market position or adoption numbers until evidence supports it.

## Long-Term Direction

If APES proves useful in practice, the long-term direction is to:

- Maintain a stable standard with predictable release cadence.
- Curate a library of real, anonymized case studies.
- Provide reference tooling guidance without owning the tooling.
- Feed learnings back into the standard via ADRs and RFCs.
